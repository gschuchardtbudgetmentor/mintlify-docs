## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **g. Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources**
>
> This section of the proposal is used to assess the adequacy of the resources available to perform the effort proposed to satisfy both the Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts review criteria. Proposers should describe only those resources that are directly applicable. Proposers should include an aggregated description of the internal and external resources (both physical and personnel) that the organization and its collaborators, and subrecipients will provide to the project, should it be funded. Such information must be provided in this section, in lieu of other parts of the proposal (e.g., Budget Justification, Project Description). The description should be narrative in nature and must not include any quantifiable financial information. Reviewers will evaluate the information during the merit review process and the cognizant NSF Program Officer will review it for programmatic and technical sufficiency.
>
> Although these resources are not considered voluntary committed cost sharing as defined in 2 CFR § 200.1, the Foundation does expect that the resources identified in the Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources section will be provided, or made available, should the proposal be funded. Chapter VII.B.1 specifies procedures for use by the recipient when there are postaward changes to objectives, scope, or methods/procedures.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### g. Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources

This section of the proposal is used to assess the adequacy of the resources available to perform the effort proposed to satisfy both the Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts review criteria.

<Tip>
  Plain English: This is where you prove you actually have what you need to do the work — lab space, equipment, shared cores, computing, library access, partner facilities, and the personnel who will support the project (but who aren't separately paid by the grant).
</Tip>

Proposers should describe only those resources that are directly applicable.

<Warning>
  Don't dump your university's whole capability statement here. Stick to what's actually relevant to this project.
</Warning>

Proposers should include an aggregated description of the internal and external resources (both physical and personnel) that the organization and its collaborators, and subrecipients will provide to the project, should it be funded.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Include resources from collaborators and subrecipients too — not just your own institution.
</Tip>

The description should be narrative in nature and must not include any quantifiable financial information.

<Danger>
  **No dollar amounts.** This section is narrative only. Putting financial values here can be treated as unauthorized voluntary committed cost sharing — which violates NSF policy.
</Danger>

Although these resources are not considered voluntary committed cost sharing as defined in 2 CFR § 200.1, the Foundation does expect that the resources identified in the Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources section will be provided, or made available, should the proposal be funded.

<Warning>
  Whatever you list here, NSF expects you to actually deliver if funded. Don't oversell facilities or commit personnel time you can't back up.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Use this section to describe **unfunded collaborators** (people contributing intellectually but not on the budget). They get a letter of collaboration in supplementary docs and a mention here.
</Note>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### g. Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources

<Tip>
  Plain English: A brag sheet about what your institution (and any collaborators or subrecipients) brings to the table — physical space, equipment, computing, personnel — to prove you can actually pull this off.
</Tip>

The description should be narrative in nature and must not include any quantifiable financial information.

<Danger>
  Write this as a **narrative**. Do **not** include dollar amounts or anything that looks like a financial figure. NSF treats this section as cost-sharing if you accidentally quantify resources in dollars, which can violate NSF's cost-sharing policy.
</Danger>

<Tip>
  Plain English: Say "Dr. Smith's wet lab provides a confocal microscope and 600 sq ft of bench space" — NOT "Dr. Smith's lab contributes \$250,000 worth of equipment and space."
</Tip>

<Warning>
  This is also where you document unfunded collaborators (people helping but not paid by this budget). Pair the description here with their Letters of Collaboration in the supplementary documents.
</Warning>

<Check>
  Although these are not formal cost sharing, NSF expects you to actually provide the resources you list here if the proposal is funded. If something changes after award, you have to follow the post-award change-of-scope procedures.
</Check>
