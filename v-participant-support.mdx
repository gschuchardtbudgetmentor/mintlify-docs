## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(v) Participant Support (Line F on the Proposal Budget)**
>
> This budget category refers to direct costs for items such as **stipends or subsistence allowances, travel allowances, and registration fees** paid to or on behalf of participants or trainees (**but not employees**) in connection with NSF-sponsored conferences or training projects.
>
> Any additional categories of participant support costs other than those described in 2 CFR § 200.1 (such as incentives, gifts, souvenirs, t-shirts, and memorabilia), must be justified in the budget justification, and such costs will be closely scrutinized by NSF.
>
> **Speakers and trainers generally are not considered participants** and should not be included in this section of the budget. However, if the primary purpose of the individual's attendance is learning/receiving training, then costs may be included here.
>
> For some educational projects at local school districts, the participants being trained are employees. In such cases, costs must be classified as participant support if payment is made through a stipend or training allowance method. The school district must have an accounting mechanism (sub-account code) to differentiate between regular salary and stipend payments.
>
> Stipends/per diem/subsistence allowances must be reasonable, in conformance with the policy of the proposing organization, and limited to days of attendance plus actual travel time.
>
> Where meals or lodgings are furnished without charge or at nominal cost (e.g., part of the registration fee), the per diem must be correspondingly reduced.
>
> Local participants may participate in meals/coffee breaks, but funds may not be used to pay per diem for local participants.
>
> Costs related to an NSF-sponsored conference (venue rental, catering, supplies, etc.) secured through a service agreement/contract should be budgeted on **G.6 Other Direct Costs**, not Participant Support.
>
> Funds may be requested for participant travel — economy class \+ U.S.-Flag carrier rules apply.
>
> The number of participants to be supported must be entered in the parentheses on the proposal budget. Participant support costs must be specified, itemized, and justified.
>
> **Indirect costs (F&A) are not usually allowed on participant support costs** unless the recipient's current Federally approved indirect cost rate agreement provides for it.
>
> Participant support costs must be **accounted for separately** if an award is made.
>
> This section may **not** be used for incentive payments to research subjects — those go on **G.6 Other Direct Costs** under "Human subject payments."

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (v) Participant Support

<Tip>
  Plain English: Line F is money for **participants and trainees** — people who come to your NSF-sponsored conference, workshop, REU, RET, or training. NOT employees doing work for you, NOT research subjects, NOT speakers/trainers.
</Tip>

<Check>
  **What counts as participant support:**

  - Stipends to trainees / REU students / conference attendees
  - Subsistence (per diem) for participants
  - Travel allowances for participants
  - Registration fees paid for participants
</Check>

<Danger>
  **Participant support is exempt from indirect costs (excluded from MTDC)** in most rate agreements. This makes Line F a powerful budget tool — but only if you classify correctly. Misclassifying speaker/employee costs as participant support is a common audit finding.
</Danger>

#### Who is NOT a participant

<Warning>
  - **Employees of your institution** — they go in salaries/wages
  - **Speakers / trainers** who are there to deliver content — go in G.3 Consultant Services or salaries
  - **Research subjects** receiving incentive payments — go in G.6 Other Direct Costs
  - **Vendors** (catering, venue) — go in G.6 Other Direct Costs
</Warning>

<Note>
  Exception: If a speaker/trainer is primarily attending **to learn** (e.g., the trainer is also a trainee in another sense), their costs can go in Participant Support. Use judgment and document.
</Note>

#### School District Educator Programs

<Tip>
  Plain English: For projects training K-12 teachers, the teachers are technically employees of the school district — but if they're paid via stipend/training allowance (not regular salary), they go in Participant Support. The school district needs a sub-account to keep this separate from regular payroll.
</Tip>

#### Per Diem Rules

<Warning>
  - Stipends/per diem limited to days of attendance \+ actual travel time
  - If meals are provided (e.g., included in registration), **reduce** the per diem accordingly
  - **No per diem for local participants** — they can eat the provided meals but can't get per diem
</Warning>

#### Itemization Required

<Check>
  On the budget, enter the **# of participants** in the parentheses on Line F. In the budget justification, itemize: stipend rate, # participants × # days, travel, registration. NSF will scrutinize "other" categories like gifts, t-shirts, souvenirs.
</Check>

#### Separate Accounting Required

<Danger>
  If awarded, participant support funds **must be accounted for separately** in your institution's accounting system. **You cannot rebudget OUT of participant support into other categories without prior NSF written approval** — that's a hard rule (2 CFR § 200.456).
</Danger>
