## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **Mandatory Cost Sharing**
>
> Mandatory cost sharing will only be required for NSF programs when explicitly authorized by the NSF Director, the NSB, or legislation. A complete listing of NSF programs that require cost sharing is available on the NSF website.
>
> In these programs, cost sharing requirements will be clearly identified in the solicitation and must be included on **Line M** of the proposal budget.
>
> For purposes of budget preparation, the **cumulative cost sharing amount must be entered on Line M of the first year's budget**. Should an award be made, the organization's cost sharing commitment, as specified on the first year's approved budget, must be met prior to the award end date.
>
> Such cost sharing will be considered as an **eligibility, rather than a review criterion**. Proposers are advised not to exceed the mandatory cost sharing level or amount specified in the solicitation.
>
> When mandatory cost sharing is included on Line M and accepted by the Foundation, the commitment of funds becomes **legally binding and is subject to audit**. When applicable, the estimated value of any in-kind contributions also should be included on Line M.
>
> An explanation of the source, nature, amount, and availability of any proposed cost sharing must be provided in the budget justification.
>
> Contributions derived from other Federal funds or counted as cost sharing toward projects of another Federal agency **must not** be counted towards meeting the specific cost sharing requirements of the NSF award.
>
> Failure to provide the level of cost sharing required by the NSF solicitation and reflected in the NSF award budget may result in **termination of the NSF award, disallowance of award costs, and/or refund of award funds to NSF**.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### Mandatory Cost Sharing

<Tip>
  Plain English: A small number of NSF programs **require** cost sharing — usually because of legislation (e.g., I/UCRC, EPSCoR Track-2 in certain cases, ERC at one point, etc.). When required, the solicitation says so explicitly and Line M unlocks.
</Tip>

<Check>
  When cost sharing is mandatory, the solicitation will:

  - State the requirement explicitly
  - Specify the amount or percentage
  - Tell you it goes on Line M

  Check the official NSF list of programs requiring cost sharing on nsf.gov.
</Check>

#### How to Budget Mandatory Cost Sharing

<Warning>
  - Enter the **total cumulative cost share** on **Line M of Year 1 only** — do not spread across years.
  - The total must equal what the solicitation requires.
  - **Do NOT exceed** the required amount. Over-promising = problem.
</Warning>

#### Eligibility, Not a Scoring Criterion

<Tip>
  Plain English: Reviewers don't score you higher for putting more cost share. It's a pass/fail eligibility check: meet the requirement → eligible. Miss it → ineligible.
</Tip>

#### Legal Binding \+ Audit

<Danger>
  Once accepted by NSF, cost sharing on Line M is **legally binding**. If you don't deliver:

  - NSF can terminate the award
  - NSF can disallow costs
  - You may have to refund money

  Treat the commitment like a real obligation, not a marketing statement.
</Danger>

#### What Counts as Cost Share

<Check>
  Allowable sources for the match:

  - Institutional funds (non-Federal)
  - Third-party contributions (in-kind, with valuation)
  - Unrecovered indirect costs (only with prior NSF approval — see 2 CFR § 200.306)
  - Donated equipment, space, supplies (valued at fair market)
</Check>

#### What Does NOT Count

<Danger>
  **You cannot use** Federal funds (from NSF or any other agency) as cost share. You also can't double-count the same dollars as cost share for two different awards. Both are explicit policy violations.
</Danger>

#### Justification

<Note>
  Your budget justification must spell out: source, nature (cash vs in-kind), amount, and availability of every cost share component. Vague "institutional commitment of \$500K" without a source breakdown won't fly.
</Note>

#### In-Kind Contributions

<Tip>
  Plain English: If a third party (a company, another non-profit) is contributing equipment, personnel time, or services, value it at fair market and include on Line M. Get a letter of commitment from them documenting the value.
</Tip>
