## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **Voluntary Committed and Uncommitted Cost Sharing**
>
> As stipulated in 2 CFR § 200.1, **"Voluntary committed cost sharing means cost sharing specifically pledged on a voluntary basis in the proposal's budget or the Federal award on the part of the non-Federal entity and that becomes a binding requirement of Federal award."**
>
> As such, to be considered voluntary committed cost sharing, the amount must appear on the NSF proposal budget and be specifically identified in the approved NSF budget.
>
> **Unless required by NSF (Mandatory Cost Sharing), inclusion of voluntary committed cost sharing is prohibited** and Line M on the proposal budget will not be available for use by the proposer.
>
> NSF Program Officers are not authorized to impose or encourage mandatory cost sharing unless such requirements are explicitly included in the program solicitation.
>
> In order for NSF and its reviewers to assess the scope of a proposed project, all organizational resources necessary for, and available to, a project must be described in the **Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources** section of the proposal.
>
> While not required by NSF, the recipient may, at its own discretion, continue to contribute **voluntary uncommitted cost sharing** to NSF-sponsored projects. These resources are not auditable by NSF and should not be included in the proposal budget or budget justification.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### Voluntary Committed and Uncommitted Cost Sharing

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF basically banned proposers from voluntarily promising matching funds. Why? Because voluntary cost sharing in the past put smaller, less-resourced institutions at a disadvantage. So unless the solicitation **requires** cost sharing, you cannot offer any.
</Tip>

<Danger>
  **Voluntary committed cost sharing is PROHIBITED** unless explicitly required by the solicitation. Line M on the budget will be locked. Don't try to imply matching funds in your justification either — that's still prohibited.
</Danger>

#### Two Types of Voluntary Cost Sharing

<Check>
  - **Voluntary Committed** = pledged in writing in the budget. Becomes legally binding if awarded. **PROHIBITED** unless solicitation requires.
  - **Voluntary Uncommitted** = resources your institution provides at its discretion without a written pledge. Not auditable. **Allowed** but do NOT put in the budget or justification.
</Check>

#### Where Resources Go Instead

<Tip>
  Plain English: Your institutional resources (lab space, equipment, computing facilities, key personnel time not paid by the grant) should be described in the **Facilities, Equipment, and Other Resources** section of the proposal — narratively, with NO dollar amounts.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Do NOT put dollar values in the Facilities, Equipment, and Other Resources section. The whole point is that it's not auditable cost sharing — it's an aggregate description.
</Warning>

#### Program Officer Pressure

<Danger>
  If a Program Officer encourages or implies you should add cost sharing during pre-submission discussions, **that's against NSF policy** unless the solicitation requires it. Document the conversation and consult your SPO.
</Danger>

<Note>
  Voluntary uncommitted cost sharing — the work and resources your institution naturally provides — happens on every grant. You just don't formalize it. NSF assumes institutions will support successful projects.
</Note>
