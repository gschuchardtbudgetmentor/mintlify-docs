## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(iii) Collaborators & Other Affiliations Information (Single Copy Document)**
>
> The following information regarding collaborators and other affiliations (COA) must be separately provided for each individual identified as a senior/key person on the project. The COA information must be provided through use of the COA template.
>
> There are five separate categories of information which correspond to the five tables in the COA template:
>
> **Table 1:** List the individual's last name, first name, middle initial, and organizational affiliation in the last 12 months.
>
> **Table 2:** List names for whom a personal, family, or business relationship would otherwise preclude their service as a reviewer. Specify the type of relationship involved.
>
> **Table 3:** List names and organizational affiliations for: the individual's Ph.D. advisors; and all of the individual's Ph.D. thesis advisees.
>
> **Table 4:** List names and organizational affiliations for: co-authors on any book, article, report, abstract or paper with collaboration in the last 48 months (publication date may be later); and collaborators on projects (funded awards, graduate research, etc.) in the last 48 months.
>
> **Table 5:** List editorial board, editor-in-chief, and co-editors with whom the individual interacts. An editor-in-chief must list the entire editorial board.
>
> - Editorial Board: name(s) of editor-in-chief and journal in the past 24 months;
> - Other co-Editors of journal or collections with whom the individual has directly interacted in the last 24 months.
>
> The template is fillable, but the content and format requirements **must not be altered**. Do not change column sizes or font type. Rows may be inserted as needed.
>
> This information is used to manage reviewer selection.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (iii) Collaborators & Other Affiliations (COA)

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF uses this list to figure out who **shouldn't** review your proposal — anyone you've collaborated with, advised, were advised by, or have a personal/business relationship with would be a conflict of interest.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  COA is a **Single-Copy Document** — it's for NSF internal use only. Reviewers don't see it. Submit one per senior/key person.
</Warning>

<Danger>
  **You must use the official NSF COA Excel template.** Don't change column widths, fonts, or layout. NSF auto-parses these files; breaking the format breaks the parser and your proposal can be returned without review. You can add rows; that's it.
</Danger>

#### Table 1 — Affiliations (last 12 months)

<Tip>
  Plain English: Every organization you've been affiliated with in the past 12 months.
</Tip>

#### Table 2 — Personal/Family/Business Relationships

<Tip>
  Plain English: People who can't be your reviewer because of a personal, family, or business tie. Specify what the relationship is.
</Tip>

#### Table 3 — PhD Advisors and Advisees

<Tip>
  Plain English: Everyone who advised you for your PhD, and everyone you've advised through their PhD — your entire career, not just recent years.
</Tip>

<Note>
  No time limit on Table 3. Advisor/advisee relationships are lifelong conflicts in NSF's eyes.
</Note>

#### Table 4 — Co-authors and Collaborators (last 48 months)

<Tip>
  Plain English: Anyone you've co-authored with or worked on a funded project with in the last 4 years. Use the **collaboration date**, not the publication date.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  4 years is a longer window than most people remember. Pull your CV, recent papers, conference abstracts, and grant collaborators to make sure you're not missing anyone.
</Warning>

#### Table 5 — Editorial Relationships (last 24 months)

<Tip>
  Plain English: If you've been on an editorial board, list the editor-in-chief and journal. If you ARE an editor-in-chief, list the **entire editorial board**. Plus any co-editors you've directly worked with on a journal or collection.
</Tip>

<Check>
  COA tables don't need to be alphabetized or sorted — just complete and accurate.
</Check>
