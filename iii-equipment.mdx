## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(iii) Equipment (Line D on the Proposal Budget)**
>
> **Equipment** is defined as tangible personal property (including information technology systems) having a useful life of more than one year and a per-unit acquisition cost which equals or exceeds the lesser of the capitalization level established by the proposer for financial statement purposes, **or \$5,000**.
>
> It is important to note that the acquisition cost of equipment includes modifications, attachments, and accessories necessary to make an item of equipment usable for the purpose for which it will be purchased.
>
> Items of needed equipment must be adequately justified, **listed individually by description and estimated cost**.
>
> Allowable items ordinarily will be limited to research equipment and apparatus not already available for the conduct of the work. **General purpose equipment such as office equipment and furnishings, and information technology equipment and systems are typically not eligible for direct cost support.**
>
> Special purpose or scientific use computers or associated hardware and software, however, may be requested as items of equipment when necessary to accomplish the project objectives and not otherwise reasonably available. Any request to support such items must be clearly disclosed in the proposal budget, justified in the budget justification, and be included in the NSF award budget.
>
> See 2 CFR §§ 200.313 and 200.439 for additional information.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (iii) Equipment

<Tip>
  Plain English: Equipment goes on **Line D**. To qualify, an item must (1) last more than one year AND (2) cost at least the lower of \$5,000 OR your institution's capitalization threshold.
</Tip>

<Check>
  **Equipment definition checklist:**

  - Useful life \> 1 year
  - Per-unit cost ≥ \$5,000 (or your institution's threshold if lower)
  - Tangible personal property (including IT systems)
  - **Includes** necessary modifications, attachments, and accessories to make it usable
</Check>

<Warning>
  If your institution's capitalization threshold is **lower** than \$5,000 (e.g., \$1,000), use that lower number. Most universities use \$5,000.
</Warning>

#### What does NOT qualify as equipment

<Danger>
  - Anything under \$5,000 → goes on **G.1 Materials and Supplies**
  - Anything with useful life \< 1 year → **G.1 Materials and Supplies**
  - General purpose office equipment/furniture → **typically unallowable**
  - General-purpose IT equipment (laptops, monitors, standard servers) → **typically unallowable** as direct equipment
</Danger>

#### Computing devices — special case

<Tip>
  Plain English: Standard computers normally can't be direct-charged. BUT — special purpose scientific computers/workstations CAN be charged as equipment if:

  1. Necessary to accomplish the project objectives
  2. Not reasonably available elsewhere
  3. Clearly disclosed in the budget
  4. Justified in the budget justification
  5. Included in the final NSF award budget
</Tip>

<Note>
  A computing device under \$5,000 is a **supply** (G.1), even if it's essential. The \$5,000\+ rule still applies.
</Note>

#### Justification requirements

<Warning>
  Each item of equipment must be:

  - **Listed individually** by description
  - **Estimated cost** shown
  - **Adequately justified** — why this exact piece, why now, why not already available
</Warning>

<Check>
  Equipment is **excluded from MTDC base** — so it does not generate indirect costs. This is one of the biggest budget structuring decisions in any NSF proposal.
</Check>

<Note>
  References: 2 CFR § 200.313 (Property Standards) and 2 CFR § 200.439 (Equipment).
</Note>
