## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(i) Biographical Sketch(es)**
>
> **(a) Senior/Key Personnel**
>
> A biographical sketch must be provided separately for each individual designated as a senior/key person through use of SciENcv (Science Experts Network Curriculum Vitae). SciENcv will produce an NSF-compliant PDF version of the biographical sketch. Senior/key personnel must prepare, save, certify, and submit these documents as part of their proposal via Research.gov or Grants.gov.
>
> Consistent with NSPM-33, individuals are required to disclose contracts associated with participation in programs sponsored by foreign governments, instrumentalities, or entities, including foreign government-sponsored talent recruitment programs. In accordance with 42 U.S.C. § 19232, individuals are prohibited from being a party in a malign foreign talent recruitment program.
>
> Individuals are reminded not to submit any personal information in the biographical sketch (home address, home phone, marital status, hobbies, etc.).
>
> The Biographical Sketch Common Form includes the following sections:
>
> **(1) Identifying Information** — Name, ORCID ID (optional), Position Title
>
> **(2) Organization and Location** — Primary organization name and location
>
> **(3) Professional Preparation** — Education and training in reverse chronological order; include all postdoc/fellowship training plus the baccalaureate. For each: organization, location, degree, start date, end date (month/year), field of study.
>
> **(4) Appointments and Positions** — All academic, professional, or institutional appointments in reverse chronological order. Must identify all domestic and foreign professional appointments **outside the primary organization** for the past 3 years.
>
> **(5) Products** — Up to 5 products most closely related to the proposed project plus up to 5 other significant products (10 total). Acceptable products include publications, websites, technologies, patents, datasets, software, etc.
>
> **(6) Certification** — Each senior/key person certifies that the information is current, accurate, and complete, and that they are not a party in a malign foreign talent recruitment program.
>
> **(b) Other Personnel**
>
> Biographical information for postdoctoral associates, other professionals, and students (research assistants) may be freeform and uploaded as a single PDF in Other Supplementary Documents.
>
> **(c) Equipment Proposals**
>
> For equipment proposals, each auxiliary user provides a short bio sketch and up to 5 publications related to the proposed acquisition.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (i) Biographical Sketch(es)

#### (a) Senior/Key Personnel

A biographical sketch must be provided separately for each individual designated as a senior/key person through use of SciENcv.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Every PI, co-PI, and senior/key person needs their own biosketch. You **must** generate it in SciENcv — you can't just upload a Word resume or a PDF you made yourself. SciENcv pulls in your ORCID data and spits out the NSF-compliant PDF.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Start your SciENcv profile early. First-time setup (linking ORCID, eRA Commons, etc.) can take a while, and SciENcv occasionally has outages right before NSF deadlines.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Plain English: The biosketch has six required parts:

  1. **Identifying Info** — name, position title, optional ORCID
  2. **Organization and Location** — your home institution
  3. **Professional Preparation** — education and training, reverse chronological, including all postdocs and your bachelor's degree
  4. **Appointments and Positions** — all positions you've held, including unpaid/honorary, and especially any foreign appointments in the last 3 years
  5. **Products** — up to 10 total (5 related \+ 5 significant), broadly defined: papers, datasets, software, patents, websites
  6. **Certification** — you personally sign that everything is true and you're not in a malign foreign talent recruitment program
</Tip>

<Danger>
  **Disclose all foreign appointments** outside your primary institution for the past 3 years. This includes visiting positions, adjunct roles, honorary titles, and talent program participation. NSF (and federal investigators) take undisclosed foreign affiliations extremely seriously.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  Do not include home address, home phone, personal email, marital status, hobbies, or any other personal info. NSF is not responsible if it gets released.
</Warning>

<Note>
  No page limit on the biosketch overall — but only the first 10 products you list will be reviewed.
</Note>

#### (b) Other Personnel

For postdoctoral associates, other professionals, and students (research assistants), biographical info can be freeform.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Postdocs, other professionals, and research assistants don't need SciENcv. You can write up a freeform bio summary, label it clearly as "Other Personnel," and upload it as a single PDF under Other Supplementary Documents.
</Tip>

#### (c) Equipment Proposals

<Note>
  Equipment proposals have a special variant: each "auxiliary user" of the requested equipment provides a short biosketch plus up to 5 closely related publications, uploaded together as a single PDF.
</Note>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (i) Biographical Sketch(es)

#### (a) Senior/Key Personnel

<Tip>
  Plain English: Every PI, Co-PI, and senior/key person needs their own NSF-compliant Biographical Sketch — and it **must** be generated through SciENcv. SciENcv exports the final PDF you upload to Research.gov / Grants.gov.
</Tip>

<Danger>
  Do **not** make your own PDF Biosketch in Word or LaTeX. Only the SciENcv-generated PDF is accepted. Submitting your own format gets the proposal returned without review.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  You must disclose all foreign government-sponsored support and any participation in foreign talent recruitment programs (NSPM-33 compliance). You're flat-out prohibited from being part of a malign foreign talent recruitment program — disclosure of legitimate foreign collaborations is OK, but malign programs disqualify you.
</Warning>

<Note>
  No personal info: skip home address, personal phone, marital status, hobbies, driver's license, etc. NSF isn't responsible if you accidentally share it.
</Note>

##### (1) Identifying Information

<Tip>
  Plain English: Name (Last, First, Middle \+ suffix), ORCID ID (optional but recommended), current position title.
</Tip>

##### (2) Organization and Location

<Tip>
  Plain English: Your primary employer's name \+ city/state/country.
</Tip>

##### (3) Professional Preparation

<Tip>
  Plain English: Your education/training history in reverse chronological order — undergrad degree onward, including every postdoc and fellowship listed separately.
</Tip>

##### (4) Appointments and Positions

<Tip>
  Plain English: All your titled positions — paid or unpaid, full-time or volunteer, adjunct, visiting, honorary — in reverse chronological order. Outside your primary org, you only need to list the last 3 years.
</Tip>

##### (5) Products

<Check>
  Two lists, up to 5 each (10 total):

  1. Products **most closely related to this proposal**
  2. Other significant products that show you can do the work
</Check>

<Tip>
  Plain English: "Products" doesn't just mean papers — patents, datasets, software, websites, models, curricula, and physical collections all count. Pick what best demonstrates you can pull off the proposed work.
</Tip>

<Note>
  You can use "et al" in the products list (unlike References Cited), but every product still needs full citation info: authors, title, date, URL, persistent identifier, journal/volume/pages where applicable.
</Note>

##### (6) Certification

<Danger>
  Each individual personally signs two certifications in SciENcv: (1) the information is accurate and complete, and (2) they are not part of a malign foreign talent recruitment program. False statements can lead to criminal prosecution.
</Danger>

#### (b) Other Personnel

<Tip>
  Plain English: Postdocs, other professionals, and student research assistants don't need SciENcv biosketches. Their bio info can be freeform — bundle it into a single PDF and upload it under "Other Supplementary Documents."
</Tip>

#### (c) Equipment Proposals

<Note>
  For equipment proposals: each auxiliary user gets a short bio sketch \+ up to 5 most-related publications. Bundle as one PDF in "Other Supplementary Documents."
</Note>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (i) Biographical Sketch(es)

#### (a) Senior/Key Personnel

A biographical sketch must be provided separately for each individual designated as a senior/key person through use of SciENcv.

<Danger>
  You **must** use SciENcv to generate the Biosketch PDF. Hand-rolled Word docs converted to PDF will be rejected. Every PI, Co-PI, and senior/key person needs their own SciENcv-generated PDF.
</Danger>

<Tip>
  Plain English: A highly specific academic résumé. NSF tells you exactly what sections to include and in what order. SciENcv enforces the format and produces the compliant PDF.
</Tip>

Individuals are required to disclose contracts associated with participation in programs sponsored by foreign governments and are prohibited from being a party in a malign foreign talent recruitment program.

<Warning>
  Foreign government contracts and foreign talent program participation must be disclosed. Failure to disclose has serious consequences — civil and potentially criminal.
</Warning>

<Danger>
  Don't include personal info (home address, personal phone/email, driver's license, marital status, hobbies, etc.). NSF will not protect this kind of information and it's not relevant to the proposal anyway.
</Danger>

The format follows the Biographical Sketch Common Form:

##### (1) Identifying Information

<Tip>
  Name, optional ORCID ID, current position title.
</Tip>

##### (2) Organization and Location

<Tip>
  Your primary employer and where it's located.
</Tip>

##### (3) Professional Preparation

<Tip>
  Plain English: Education and training in reverse chronological order — every postdoc and fellowship gets its own line, all the way back to your bachelor's degree. Include the institution, location, degree, dates, and field.
</Tip>

##### (4) Appointments and Positions

<Tip>
  Plain English: Every academic, professional, or institutional position you've held — paid or unpaid, full-time, part-time, adjunct, visiting, honorary. List in reverse chronological order.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  For positions **outside** your primary organization, you must report appointments from the past **3 years** from submission. This includes foreign appointments.
</Warning>

##### (5) Products

<Tip>
  Plain English: Up to **5 products most related** to the proposed project \+ up to **5 other significant products**. Only those 10 will be considered in review. "Products" is broader than publications — patents, datasets, software, websites, curricula, instruments, even new businesses count.
</Tip>

<Note>
  For multi-author publications, you may use one author's name and "et al" to save space.
</Note>

##### (6) Certification

<Danger>
  Each senior/key person personally certifies their Biosketch is current, accurate, and complete, AND that they're not in a malign foreign talent recruitment program. False statements can carry criminal liability.
</Danger>

#### (b) Other Personnel

<Tip>
  Plain English: Postdocs, other professionals, and students don't need SciENcv. Their bios can be free-form and uploaded as a single combined PDF under Other Supplementary Documents. Useful when their qualifications are key to the proposal's success.
</Tip>

#### (c) Equipment Proposals

<Tip>
  Plain English: If you're proposing to acquire shared equipment, include a short bio \+ up to 5 most-related publications for each auxiliary user who will rely on the equipment.
</Tip>
