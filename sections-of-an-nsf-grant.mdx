### 2. Sections of the Proposal

The sections described below represent <Tooltip tip="The required parts of every standard research proposal. Miss one and your proposal is returned without review.">the body of a research proposal</Tooltip> submitted to NSF. <Tooltip tip="If you skip a required section, NSF rejects the proposal administratively — no reviewer ever sees the science.">Failure to submit the required sections will result in the proposal not being accepted</Tooltip>, or being returned without review. See Chapter IV.B for additional information.

A full research proposal must contain the following sections. Note that the _NSF Grants.gov Application Guide_ may use different naming conventions, and sections may appear in a different order than in Research.gov — <Tooltip tip="The portal labels and ordering can look different, but the underlying content NSF wants is identical.">however, the content is the same</Tooltip>:

**1.** <Tooltip tip="The title page. Project title, budget totals, institutional info, and checkboxes for special situations.">**Cover Sheet**</Tooltip>

**2.** <Tooltip tip="A one-page pitch that must explicitly cover Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts as labeled sections.">**Project Summary**</Tooltip>

**3.** <Tooltip tip="Usually auto-generated by Research.gov from the rest of your uploaded files.">**Table of Contents**</Tooltip>

**4.** <Tooltip tip="The heart of the proposal — up to 15 pages of research plan, methods, prior results, and timeline.">**Project Description**</Tooltip>

**5.** <Tooltip tip="Your bibliography. Every claim in the Project Description needs a citation here.">**References Cited**</Tooltip>

**6.** <Tooltip tip="How much money you need and a line-by-line justification of every dollar. Budget Mentor specializes in this part.">**Budget and Budget Justification**</Tooltip>

**7.** <Tooltip tip="Proof your institution has the labs, equipment, computing, and support staff to actually do the work.">**Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources**</Tooltip>

**8.** <Tooltip tip="Mandatory profiles for every PI and Co-PI — track record + conflicts of interest.">**Senior/Key Personnel Documents**</Tooltip>, which include:

- (i) <Tooltip tip="A highly structured academic résumé in NSF's required SciENcv format.">Biographical Sketch(es)</Tooltip>
- (ii) <Tooltip tip="Every grant you have or have pending — proves you have the effort to do this project and aren't double-billing.">Current and Pending (Other) Support</Tooltip>
- (iii) <Tooltip tip="A list of co-authors, advisors, and recent collaborators so NSF can avoid conflicted reviewers. Submitted as a Single-Copy Document.">Collaborators and Other Affiliations</Tooltip>
- (iv) <Tooltip tip="Up to 5 examples of work that integrates research with education or broader societal impact.">Synergistic Activities</Tooltip>

**9.** <Tooltip tip="Extra documents required only if your project triggers them (postdocs, data, fieldwork, etc.).">**Special Information and Supplementary Documentation**</Tooltip>, which includes:

- (i) <Tooltip tip="Required if your budget pays any postdoc or graduate student. 1 page maximum.">Mentoring Plan (if applicable)</Tooltip>
- (ii) <Tooltip tip="Up to 2 pages explaining how you'll store, protect, and share research data.">Data Management and Sharing Plan</Tooltip>

The proposal preparation instructions for <Tooltip tip="Special grant types: rapid-response, exploratory, infrastructure, etc. They have shorter, looser requirements than a standard proposal.">Planning, RAPID, EAGER, RAISE, GOALI, Ideas Lab, FASED, Conference, Equipment, Travel, Center, and Research Infrastructure proposal types</Tooltip> may deviate from the above content requirements.

**All proposals submitted to NSF will be reviewed using** <Tooltip tip="Intellectual Merit (is the science good?) and Broader Impacts (does it benefit society?). Every reviewer scores you on both.">the two NSB-approved merit review criteria</Tooltip> **described in greater length in Chapter III.**
