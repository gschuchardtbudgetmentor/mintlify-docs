## Information Key

## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

> ### **(a) Senior/Key Personnel Salaries & Wages Policy**
>
> NSF regards research as one of the normal functions of faculty members at institutions of higher education. Compensation for time normally spent on research within the term of appointment is deemed to be included within the faculty member's regular organizational salary.
>
> As a general policy, NSF limits the salary compensation requested in the proposal budget for each senior/key person to no more than two months of their regular salary in any one year. This limit includes salary compensation received from all NSF-funded grants. This effort must be documented in accordance with 2 CFR § 200, Subpart E, including 2 CFR § 200.430(i). If anticipated, any compensation for such personnel in excess of two months must be disclosed in the proposal budget, justified in the budget justification, and must be specifically approved by NSF in the award notice budget.
>
> It is the organization's responsibility to define and consistently apply the term "year", and to specify this definition in the budget justification.
>
> Under normal rebudgeting authority, a recipient can internally approve an increase or decrease in person months devoted to the project after an award is made, even if doing so results in salary support for a senior/key person exceeding the two-month salary policy. No prior approval from NSF is necessary unless the rebudgeting would cause the objectives or scope of the project to change.
>
> These same general principles apply to other types of non-academic organizations.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (a) Senior/Key Personnel Salaries & Wages Policy

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF will only pay each senior/key person up to **2 months of their salary per year** across all NSF awards combined. Going over requires NSF's explicit approval.
</Tip>

<Danger>
  The **2-month rule** is the most-violated NSF budget rule. It includes salary from **every** active NSF grant the PI has — not just this one. If you already have 2 months of summer salary on another NSF award, you can't request any more on this one without specific approval.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  You must define what "year" means (academic year? calendar year? 12-month appointment?) in your budget justification and use that definition consistently.
</Warning>

<Check>
  Faculty research time is presumed to already be part of regular faculty salary. NSF won't pay you for time you're already paid to do research on.
</Check>

#### Going over 2 months

<Note>
  If you need more than 2 months in any year:

  1. Show the excess clearly on the budget line
  2. Justify it in the budget justification
  3. NSF must specifically approve it in the award notice budget
</Note>

#### After the award is made

<Tip>
  Plain English: After you have the award, your institution can rebudget person-months up or down — even exceeding 2 months — without NSF approval, as long as the scope/objectives don't change. Scope change = NSF prior approval required.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  "Same principles apply to non-academic organizations" means industry, nonprofits, and other proposers also need to define their year and stay within reasonable salary policy.
</Warning>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (a) Senior/Key Personnel Salaries & Wages Policy

NSF regards research as one of the normal functions of faculty members at institutions of higher education. Compensation for time normally spent on research within the term of appointment is deemed to be included within the faculty member's regular organizational salary.

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF assumes research is already part of your day job as a faculty member, so your base salary already covers it. NSF will only pay you on top of that for a limited amount of time.
</Tip>

As a general policy, NSF limits the salary compensation requested in the proposal budget for each senior/key person to no more than two months of their regular salary in any one year.

<Danger>
  **The "2-Month Rule"**: NSF will only pay each senior/key person up to **2 months of salary per year**, total, **across all NSF grants combined**. Not 2 months per grant.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  Your institution defines what "a year" means (calendar year, academic year \+ summer, etc.) — but you must define it the same way every time and write the definition into the budget justification.
</Warning>

If anticipated, any compensation for such personnel in excess of two months must be disclosed in the proposal budget, justified in the budget justification, and must be specifically approved by NSF in the award notice budget.

<Tip>
  Plain English: If you genuinely need to ask for more than 2 months (rare), you have to (1) show it on the budget, (2) explain why in the budget justification, and (3) get NSF to specifically approve it in the award notice. You can't sneak past the limit.
</Tip>

Under normal rebudgeting authority, a recipient can internally approve an increase or decrease in person months devoted to the project after an award is made, even if doing so results in salary support for a senior/key person exceeding the two-month salary policy.

<Note>
  After an award is made, you can shift effort around internally — even past the 2-month line — as long as scope and objectives don't change. If scope changes, you need NSF prior approval.
</Note>

<Check>
  Same principles apply to non-academic organizations (companies, non-profits, etc.), not just universities.
</Check>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (a) Senior/Key Personnel Salaries & Wages Policy

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF assumes research is already part of your normal faculty job, so your base 9-month academic salary already covers some research. NSF will pay you for **summer salary** or other additional effort — but with limits.
</Tip>

<Danger>
  **The 2-month rule:** No senior/key person can request more than **2 months of salary across all NSF grants combined** in any one year. Not 2 months per grant — 2 months total across every NSF award you hold.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  Your institution defines what counts as a "year" (calendar, academic, fiscal). Pick one and state it clearly in the budget justification — and use it consistently.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Plain English: If you genuinely need more than 2 months on this project, you can ask — but you must:

  1. Show it in the proposal budget
  2. Justify it in the budget justification
  3. Get explicit NSF approval in the award notice
</Tip>

<Note>
  Post-award, you can rebudget internally to spend more than 2 months without NSF prior approval, as long as the scope/objectives of the project don't change.
</Note>

<Check>
  This policy applies to non-academic organizations too, not just universities.
</Check>
