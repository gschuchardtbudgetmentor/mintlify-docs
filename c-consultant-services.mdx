## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(c) Consultant Services (Line G.3)**
>
> The proposal budget may request costs for professional and consultant services. Professional and consultant services are services rendered by persons who are members of a particular profession or possess a special skill, and who are **not officers or employees of the proposing organization**.
>
> Costs of professional and consultant services are allowable when reasonable in relation to the services rendered and when **not contingent upon recovery of costs from the Federal government**.
>
> Anticipated services must be justified, and information furnished on each individual's:
>
> - expertise
> - primary organizational affiliation
> - normal daily compensation rate
> - number of days of expected service
>
> Consultants' travel costs, including subsistence, may be included.
>
> If requested, the proposer must be able to justify that the proposed rate of pay is reasonable.
>
> Additional information on the allowability of consultant or professional service costs is available in 2 CFR § 200.459.
>
> In addition to other provisions required by the proposing organization, all contracts made under the NSF award must contain the applicable provisions identified in 2 CFR § 200 Appendix II.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (c) Consultant Services

<Tip>
  Plain English: Line G.3 is for paying outside experts — people who are NOT your employees but have a skill you need. Statisticians, technical advisors, evaluators, content experts, etc.
</Tip>

<Check>
  **For each consultant, the budget justification must include:**

  - Name (or "TBD" with role description)
  - Expertise / why you need them
  - Primary organizational affiliation
  - Normal daily compensation rate
  - Number of days of expected service
  - Total compensation = rate × days
</Check>

<Warning>
  Consultant fees **cannot be contingent on getting the federal grant**. The consultant can't say "I'll work for free if you don't win, otherwise pay me \$X." That voids allowability.
</Warning>

<Danger>
  Internal employees of your institution **cannot** be paid as consultants on this grant — they must be paid as salaries (Lines A/B). This is one of the most common compliance errors.
</Danger>

#### Reasonableness Test

<Tip>
  Plain English: NSF (and auditors) can ask you to justify the daily rate. A defensible rate is one that:

  - Matches the consultant's regular external rate
  - Is competitive with similar consultants
  - Is documented (e.g., consultant's standard fee schedule, prior contracts)
</Tip>

#### Consultant Travel

<Note>
  Travel costs for consultants (airfare, lodging, per diem) go on **Line G.3 along with their fees**, not on Line E. Itemize them.
</Note>

#### Distinguishing Consultant vs Subaward

<Warning>
  - **Consultant** = individual providing a service, paid by day/hour
  - **Subaward** = another organization conducting a portion of the scientific work, with their own PI/team

  Use 2 CFR § 200.331 to make the call. If unsure, talk to your SPO.
</Warning>

<Check>
  Consultant services are included in the **MTDC base** — they generate indirect costs.
</Check>
