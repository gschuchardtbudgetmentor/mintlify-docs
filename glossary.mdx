## A

<a id="awardee" />

### 🏛️ Awardee

The legal entity (usually a university, college, non-profit, or company) that receives the NSF grant. The PI works _for_ the awardee — the awardee signs the agreement and is legally responsible for the funds. Also called the **recipient** or **grantee**.

<a id="aor" />

### ✍️ AOR (Authorized Organizational Representative)

The person at your institution who is legally allowed to submit proposals and sign agreements on behalf of the awardee. Usually someone in the Sponsored Programs Office (SPO) or Office of Sponsored Research (OSR). Not the PI.

## B

<a id="broader-impacts" />

### 🌍 Broader Impacts

One of NSF's two merit review criteria. Describes how your project will benefit society — education, outreach, diversity, infrastructure, public engagement. Required in the Project Summary and Project Description.

## C

<a id="co-pi" />

### 👥 Co-PI (Co-Principal Investigator)

A collaborator who shares scientific leadership of the project with the PI. NSF allows up to 4 Co-PIs per proposal. Each must be listed on the Cover Sheet and submit a Biosketch and Current & Pending Support.

<a id="cover-sheet" />

### 📋 Cover Sheet

The first page of an NSF proposal. Generated automatically in Research.gov from data you enter (PI info, institution, title, funding amount, dates, program). You don't write it from scratch — you fill out fields.

## D

<a id="duns-uei" />

### 🔢 UEI (Unique Entity Identifier)

The 12-character ID assigned by SAM.gov that replaced the old DUNS number in 2022. Your institution has one; you'll need it for the Cover Sheet.

## F

<a id="funding-opportunity" />

### 📣 Funding Opportunity

An NSF program solicitation, Dear Colleague Letter (DCL), or the standing Program Description that you're applying to. Determines the rules, deadlines, and review criteria for your proposal.

## I

<a id="intellectual-merit" />

### 🧠 Intellectual Merit

NSF's other merit review criterion. Describes the potential of your project to advance knowledge in its field. Required in the Project Summary and Project Description.

## N

<a id="nsf-id" />

### 🆔 NSF ID

The personal account ID NSF assigns to every PI, Co-PI, and other senior personnel. Required for everyone listed on the Cover Sheet. Get one at [research.gov](https://research.gov).

## P

<a id="pappg" />

### 📘 PAPPG (Proposal & Award Policies & Procedures Guide)

NSF's master rulebook for proposals and awards. The current version is **NSF 24-1**, effective for proposals due on or after May 20, 2024. This entire guide translates PAPPG into plain English.

<a id="pi" />

### 👤 PI (Principal Investigator)

The lead scientist responsible for the project. Listed first on the Cover Sheet. Signs off on technical content, manages the budget, submits annual reports.

<a id="program-officer" />

### 🧑‍💼 Program Officer (PO)

The NSF staff member who manages a specific funding program. They decide which proposals get reviewed in which panel, and they make the final funding recommendation. Talk to them _before_ you submit.

## R

<a id="research-gov" />

### 🌐 Research.gov

NSF's online proposal submission system. Where you create the Cover Sheet, upload PDFs, and submit. Replaced FastLane in 2023.

## S

<a id="solicitation" />

### 📜 Solicitation

A specific NSF call for proposals with its own number (e.g., NSF 24-512), deadlines, and rules. Always read the solicitation _and_ PAPPG — solicitation rules override PAPPG when they conflict.

<a id="spo" />

### 🏢 SPO / OSR (Sponsored Programs Office / Office of Sponsored Research)

Your institution's grants office. They review your proposal, sign off as the AOR, and submit it through Research.gov. Loop them in _early_ — most have internal deadlines 3–5 business days before NSF's.

## U

<a id="unsolicited" />

### 📭 Unsolicited Proposal

A proposal submitted to NSF's general Program Description rather than a specific solicitation. Most NSF proposals are unsolicited. Different rules apply (mainly: PAPPG governs in full, no solicitation-specific overrides).
