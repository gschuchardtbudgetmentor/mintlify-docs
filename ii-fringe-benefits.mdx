## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(ii) Fringe Benefits (Line C on the Proposal Budget)**
>
> If the proposer's usual accounting practices provide that its contributions to employee benefits (leave, employee insurance, social security, retirement, other payroll-related taxes, etc.) be treated as direct costs, NSF award funds may be requested to fund fringe benefits as a direct cost.
>
> These are typically determined by application of a calculated fringe benefit rate for a particular class of employee (full time or part-time) applied to the salaries and wages requested. They also may be paid based on actual costs for individual employees if that institutional policy has been approved by the cognizant Federal agency.
>
> See 2 CFR § 200.431 for the definition and allowability of inclusion of fringe benefits on a proposal budget.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (ii) Fringe Benefits

<Tip>
  Plain English: Fringe = the employer-paid stuff that comes with employing someone — health insurance, retirement contributions, social security, payroll taxes, paid leave. You request it as a direct cost on **Line C** by applying your institution's fringe rate to the salaries/wages on Lines A and B.
</Tip>

<Check>
  Most institutions have multiple **fringe benefit rates** depending on employee class:

  - Full-time faculty/staff (often 25-35%\+)
  - Part-time / temporary employees (often lower)
  - Postdocs (often a separate rate)
  - Grad students (often very low or zero)
  - Undergrads / hourly (often very low)
</Check>

<Warning>
  Don't guess the rate. Get the current federally-negotiated fringe rates from your Sponsored Programs Office. Using last year's rate or a wrong category can mean a shortfall you have to absorb internally.
</Warning>

#### How it works on the budget

<Tip>
  Plain English: For each employee category in your salaries lines, multiply the salary by that category's fringe rate. Sum them and that goes on Line C. Show the math in the budget justification so reviewers and NSF can verify.
</Tip>

<Note>
  Some institutions charge fringe based on **actual costs per employee** rather than a rate — that's allowed only if your cognizant federal agency has approved it. Most universities use rate-based fringe.
</Note>

<Danger>
  Fringe rates change annually. For multi-year budgets, escalate the fringe rate forward if your institution's rate agreement has projected future rates. Don't flat-line it across all years unless your SPO confirms.
</Danger>

<Check>
  Reference: 2 CFR § 200.431.
</Check>
