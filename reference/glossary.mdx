## A

### AOR — Authorized Organizational Representative {#aor}

The person at your institution who is legally authorized to submit proposals to NSF on the organization's behalf. Usually someone in your Sponsored Programs Office (SPO) or Office of Research. **Not you, the PI.** Only the AOR can hit the "submit" button in Research.gov. 🧑‍💼

### Award {#award}

The money NSF sends you after they decide to fund your proposal. Comes with strings attached (terms & conditions, reporting requirements, allowable cost rules). 💸

## B

### Broader Impacts {#broader-impacts}

One of NSF's two merit-review criteria. Asks: _how does your research benefit society?_ Education, outreach, broadening participation, public engagement, training the next generation — all count. 🌍

### Budget Justification {#budget-justification}

The narrative document (max **5 pages** under PAPPG 24-1) where you explain _why_ every dollar in your budget is necessary. NSF reviewers and program officers read this carefully. 📝

## C

### Co-PI / Co-Principal Investigator {#co-pi}

A collaborator who shares responsibility for the intellectual direction of the project. Listed on the Cover Sheet alongside the PI. NSF allows up to **4 co-PIs** per proposal. 🤝

### Cost Sharing {#cost-sharing}

When your institution contributes resources (cash or in-kind) toward the project beyond what NSF is paying. NSF generally **prohibits voluntary committed cost sharing** — only include it if the solicitation explicitly requires it. ⚖️

### Cover Sheet {#cover-sheet}

The first form in every NSF proposal. Collects administrative info: PI, institution, title, dates, budget total, program announcement, and certifications. Generated automatically by Research.gov from data you enter. 📄

## D

### DUNS / UEI — Unique Entity Identifier {#uei}

The 12-character ID that uniquely identifies your institution in the federal SAM.gov system. Replaced the old DUNS number in April 2022. 🏛️

### Direct Costs {#direct-costs}

Expenses you can directly attribute to the project: salaries, equipment, travel, supplies, etc. Contrast with **indirect costs**. 💵

## E

### EPSCoR — Established Program to Stimulate Competitive Research {#epscor}

A program that supports research in states/territories that historically receive less federal R&D funding. Some solicitations are EPSCoR-only. 🗺️

## F

### F&A — Facilities and Administrative Costs {#fa}

The formal name for **indirect costs** (a.k.a. overhead). The percentage your institution charges to cover shared infrastructure (libraries, utilities, admin staff). Negotiated with your cognizant federal agency. 🏢

### Fringe Benefits {#fringe-benefits}

Employer-paid costs on top of salary: health insurance, retirement, payroll taxes, etc. Charged as a percentage of salary using your institution's federally negotiated rate. 🩺

## G

### GPG — Grant Proposal Guide {#gpg}

The old name for what is now Part I of the **PAPPG**. You'll still hear veterans say "GPG." 📘

## I

### Indirect Costs {#indirect-costs}

Same thing as **F&A costs**. Overhead. The portion of your award that goes to institutional infrastructure, not directly to your project. 🏢

### Intellectual Merit {#intellectual-merit}

NSF's other merit-review criterion (the partner to **Broader Impacts**). Asks: _does this research advance knowledge in the field?_ 🧠

## N

### NSF — National Science Foundation {#nsf}

The US federal agency that funds basic research and education in non-medical sciences and engineering. ~\$9B annual budget. 🔬

### NSF ID {#nsf-id}

Your personal 9-digit identifier in Research.gov. Every PI, co-PI, and senior personnel needs one. Free to create. 🆔

## P

### PAPPG — Proposal & Award Policies & Procedures Guide {#pappg}

The NSF rulebook. Tells you what to put in your proposal, how to format it, what's allowable, and how NSF will review it. The current version is **NSF 24-1**, effective May 20, 2024. 📕

### PI — Principal Investigator {#pi}

You (probably). The person scientifically and intellectually responsible for the project. 🧑‍🔬

### Project Description {#project-description}

The core scientific narrative. Max **15 pages** unless the solicitation says otherwise. Must include sections on Results from Prior NSF Support, Intellectual Merit, and Broader Impacts. 📑

### Project Summary {#project-summary}

The 1-page overview that goes at the front of your proposal. Must contain three labeled sections: **Overview**, **Intellectual Merit**, **Broader Impacts**. ✍️

## R

### Research.gov {#research-gov}

NSF's online proposal submission and award management portal. Where you build, submit, and manage your proposal. 🌐

### RUI — Research in Undergraduate Institutions {#rui}

A program that supports research at primarily undergraduate institutions. Has its own special proposal sections. 🎓

## S

### Senior/Key Personnel {#senior-key-personnel}

People whose contribution is essential to the project — PI, co-PIs, and other named scientists. Each must submit a Biographical Sketch, Current & Pending Support, Collaborators & Other Affiliations, and (if relevant) a Synergistic Activities document. 👥

### Solicitation {#solicitation}

A specific NSF funding opportunity (e.g., "NSF 24-567"). Solicitations can **override** PAPPG defaults — always read the solicitation first. 📢

### SPO — Sponsored Programs Office {#spo}

Your institution's office responsible for grant submissions, compliance, and post-award management. Your best friend during proposal prep. 🤝

## U

### UEI — Unique Entity Identifier {#uei-link}

See [UEI](#uei). 🆔
