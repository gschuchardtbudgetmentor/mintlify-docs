> ### **d. Project Description (including Results from Prior NSF Support)**
>
> **(i) Content**
>
> The Project Description should provide a clear statement of the work to be undertaken and must include the objectives for the period of the proposed work and expected significance; the relationship of this work to the present state of knowledge in the field, as well as to work in progress by the PI under other support.
>
> The Project Description should outline the general plan of work, including the broad design of activities to be undertaken, and, where appropriate, provide a clear description of experimental methods and procedures. Proposers should address what they want to do, why they want to do it, how they plan to do it, how they will know if they succeed, and what benefits could accrue if the project is successful. The project activities may be based on previously established and/or innovative methods and approaches, but in either case must be well justified. These issues apply to both the technical aspects of the proposal and the way in which the project may make broader contributions.
>
> The Project Description also must contain, as a separate section within the narrative, a section labeled "Broader Impacts", and "Broader Impacts" must appear as a heading on its own line. This section should provide a discussion of the broader impacts of the proposed activities. Broader impacts may be accomplished through the research itself, through the activities that are directly related to specific research projects, or through activities that are supported by, but are complementary to the project. NSF values the advancement of scientific knowledge and activities that contribute to the achievement of societally relevant outcomes. Such outcomes include, but are not limited to: full participation of women, persons with disabilities, and underrepresented minorities in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics (STEM); improved STEM education and educator development at any level; increased public scientific literacy and public engagement with science and technology; improved well-being of individuals in society; development of a diverse, globally competitive STEM workforce; increased partnerships between academia, industry, and others; improved national security; increased economic competitiveness of the U.S.; use of science and technology to inform public policy; and enhanced infrastructure for research and education. These examples of societally relevant outcomes should not be considered either comprehensive or prescriptive. Proposers may include appropriate outcomes not covered by these examples.
>
> Plans for data management and sharing of the products of research, including preservation, documentation, and sharing of data, samples, physical collections, curriculum materials and other related research and education products should be described in the Special Information and Supplementary Documentation section of the proposal (see Chapter II.D.2.i for additional instructions for preparation of this section).
>
> For proposals that include funding to an International Branch Campus of a U.S. IHE or to a foreign organization or foreign individual (including through use of a subaward or consultant arrangement), the proposer must provide the requisite explanation/justification in the project description. See Chapter I.E for additional information on the content requirements.
>
> **(ii) Page Limitations and Inclusion of Uniform Resource Locators (URLs) within the Project Description**
>
> Brevity will assist reviewers and Foundation staff in dealing effectively with proposals. Therefore, the Project Description (including Results from Prior NSF Support, which is limited to five pages) may not exceed 15 pages. Visual materials, including charts, graphs, maps, photographs, and other pictorial presentations are included in the 15-page limitation. PIs are cautioned that the Project Description must be self-contained, and that URLs must not be used because: 1) the information could circumvent page limitations; 2) the reviewers are under no obligation to view the sites; and 3) the sites could be altered or deleted between the time of submission and the time of review.
>
> Conformance to the 15-page limit will be strictly enforced and may not be exceeded unless a deviation has been specifically authorized.
>
> **(iii) Results from Prior NSF Support**
>
> The purpose of this section is to assist reviewers in assessing the quality of prior work conducted with prior or current NSF funding. If any PI or co-PI identified on the proposal has received prior NSF support including:
>
> - an award with an end date in the past five years; or
> - any current funding, including any no cost extensions,
>
> information on the award is required for each PI and co-PI, regardless of whether the support was directly related to the proposal or not. In cases where the PI or any co-PI has received more than one award (excluding amendments to existing awards), they need only report on the one award that is most closely related to the proposal. Support means salary support, as well as any other funding awarded by NSF, including research, Graduate Research Fellowship, Major Research Instrumentation, conference, equipment, travel, and center awards, etc.
>
> The following information must be provided:
>
> (a) the NSF award number, amount and period of support;
>
> (b) the title of the project;
>
> (c) a summary of the results of the completed work, including accomplishments, supported by the award. The results must be separately described under two distinct headings: Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts;
>
> (d) a listing of the publications resulting from the NSF award (a complete bibliographic citation for each publication must be provided either in this section or in the References Cited section of the proposal); if none, state "No publications were produced under this award."
>
> (e) evidence of research products and their availability, including, but not limited to: data, publications, samples, physical collections, software, and models, as described in any Data Management and Sharing Plan; and
>
> (f) if the proposal is for renewed support, a description of the relation of the completed work to the proposed work.
>
> If the project was recently awarded and therefore no new results exist, describe the major goals and broader impacts of the project. Note that the proposal may contain up to five pages to describe the results. Results may be summarized in fewer than five pages, which would give the balance of the 15 pages for the Project Description.
>
> **(iv) Unfunded Collaborations**
>
> Any substantial collaboration with individuals not included in the budget should be described in the Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources section of the proposal and documented in a letter of collaboration from each collaborator. Such letters should be provided in the supplementary documentation section of Research.gov and follow the format instructions specified in Chapter II.D.2.i. Collaborative activities that are identified in the budget should follow the instructions in Chapter II.E.3.
>
> **(v) Group Proposals**
>
> NSF encourages submission of proposals by groups of investigators; often these are submitted to carry out interdisciplinary projects. Unless stipulated in a specific program solicitation, however, such proposals will be subject to the 15-page Project Description limitation established in Section (ii) above. PIs who wish to exceed the established page limitations for the Project Description must request and receive a deviation in advance of proposal submission.
>
> **(vi) Proposals for Renewed Support**
>
> See Chapter V for guidance on preparation of renewal proposals.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### d. Project Description (including Results from Prior NSF Support)

#### (i) Content

The Project Description should provide a clear statement of the work to be undertaken and must include the objectives for the period of the proposed work and expected significance; the relationship of this work to the present state of knowledge in the field, as well as to work in progress by the PI under other support.

<Tip>
  Plain English: This is the main scientific narrative — the meat of the proposal. State the objectives, why they matter, and how this work fits into the current state of the field.
</Tip>

The Project Description should outline the general plan of work, including the broad design of activities to be undertaken, and, where appropriate, provide a clear description of experimental methods and procedures.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Reviewers want to see five things clearly answered:

  1. **What** do you want to do?
  2. **Why** does it matter?
  3. **How** will you do it (methods)?
  4. **How will you know if you succeeded** (evaluation criteria)?
  5. **What benefits** if you succeed?
</Tip>

The Project Description also must contain, as a separate section within the narrative, a section labeled "Broader Impacts", and "Broader Impacts" must appear as a heading on its own line.

<Danger>
  "Broader Impacts" must literally appear as a heading on its own line in your Project Description. Not in the Summary alone — also here. Missing this heading is a common reason for proposals to be returned without review.
</Danger>

<Tip>
  Plain English: Broader Impacts examples NSF lists include: participation of women, persons with disabilities, and underrepresented minorities in STEM; improved STEM education; public scientific literacy; improved well-being; diverse STEM workforce; academia-industry partnerships; national security; economic competitiveness; informing public policy; research infrastructure. You're not limited to these — but pick something concrete.
</Tip>

Plans for data management and sharing of the products of research... should be described in the Special Information and Supplementary Documentation section of the proposal.

<Note>
  Data Management and Sharing Plan goes in the supplementary docs, **not** in the 15-page Project Description.
</Note>

For proposals that include funding to an International Branch Campus of a U.S. IHE or to a foreign organization or foreign individual (including through use of a subaward or consultant arrangement), the proposer must provide the requisite explanation/justification in the project description.

<Warning>
  If you are sending NSF money to a foreign org or foreign individual (including through a subaward or consultant), you must justify it inside the Project Description itself.
</Warning>

#### (ii) Page Limitations and URLs

The Project Description (including Results from Prior NSF Support, which is limited to five pages) may not exceed 15 pages.

<Danger>
  Hard 15-page limit, including all charts, graphs, photos, and figures. The 5-page Results from Prior NSF Support counts inside that 15. Going over without a pre-approved deviation = proposal returned without review.
</Danger>

PIs are cautioned that the Project Description must be self-contained, and that URLs must not be used.

<Warning>
  Do not use URLs in the Project Description. NSF rejects this practice for three reasons:

  1. It circumvents the page limit.
  2. Reviewers aren't required to click them.
  3. Sites can change between submission and review.
</Warning>

#### (iii) Results from Prior NSF Support

The purpose of this section is to assist reviewers in assessing the quality of prior work conducted with prior or current NSF funding.

<Tip>
  Plain English: If any PI/co-PI has had NSF funding (current OR ending within the past 5 years), you must report on it — even if it has nothing to do with this proposal. If they've had multiple awards, pick the one most relevant to this proposal.
</Tip>

<Check>
  Each Prior NSF Support entry must include: (a) award number, amount, and dates; (b) project title; (c) results under **two distinct headings: Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts**; (d) publications (or "No publications were produced under this award."); (e) research products and availability; (f) for renewals, relationship to proposed work.
</Check>

<Note>
  You get up to 5 pages for this — but if you use less, the rest goes back into your 15-page Project Description budget.
</Note>

#### (iv) Unfunded Collaborations

<Tip>
  Plain English: If a collaborator helps you but isn't getting paid through this grant, describe their contribution in the **Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources** section and upload a letter of collaboration in supplementary documents.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Letters of collaboration must state intent to collaborate only — they cannot praise the PI or endorse the project. Letters of support (endorsements) are not allowed unless a solicitation specifically requires them.
</Warning>

#### (v) Group Proposals

<Tip>
  Plain English: A team of investigators on one proposal still gets the same 15 pages — not 15 per person. If you genuinely need more, you have to request a deviation from NSF in advance.
</Tip>

#### (vi) Proposals for Renewed Support

<Note>
  Renewal proposals follow special guidance in PAPPG Chapter V.
</Note>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### d. Project Description (including Results from Prior NSF Support)

#### (i) Content

<Tip>
  Plain English: This is the heart of your proposal. Reviewers spend most of their time here. Answer the five questions clearly:

  1. **What** do you want to do?
  2. **Why** does it matter?
  3. **How** will you do it (methods, design)?
  4. **How will you know it worked** (success metrics)?
  5. **What benefits** come from success?
</Tip>

<Danger>
  You MUST include a section explicitly labeled "Broader Impacts" as its own heading. This is non-negotiable — proposals missing this labeled section are returned without review.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  If your project funds an International Branch Campus of a U.S. university, a foreign organization, or a foreign individual (including via subaward or consultant), you must justify it inside the Project Description.
</Warning>

#### (ii) Page Limitations and URLs

The Project Description may not exceed 15 pages.

<Danger>
  Hard cap: **15 pages** for Project Description (with up to 5 of those pages used for Results from Prior NSF Support). Figures, charts, photos, and maps count toward the 15. Strictly enforced.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  No URLs in the Project Description. NSF treats embedded links as an attempt to dodge the page limit. Reviewers won't click them, and the linked content might change before review.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Plain English: Need more pages? You must request and receive an authorized deviation **before** submitting (see Single-Copy Documents → a).
</Tip>

#### (iii) Results from Prior NSF Support

<Tip>
  Plain English: If you or any Co-PI got NSF money that ended within the last 5 years OR is currently active (including no-cost extensions), you have to report on it — even if it had nothing to do with this proposal.
</Tip>

<Note>
  If you've had multiple NSF awards, you only need to report on the one most closely related to this proposal.
</Note>

For each award, provide:

- (a) NSF award number, amount, and period of support
- (b) Title of the project
- (c) Summary of results — **with separate Intellectual Merit and Broader Impacts headings**
- (d) Publications resulting from the award (or "No publications were produced under this award.")
- (e) Evidence of research products and their availability (data, software, samples, etc.)
- (f) If renewal, how completed work relates to proposed work

<Warning>
  This section is limited to 5 pages and counts inside the 15-page Project Description limit. Use as little of the 5 pages as you can — every page here is a page you don't have for the new science.
</Warning>

<Tip>
  Plain English: If your award is brand new and there are no results yet, just describe the project's goals and broader impacts.
</Tip>

#### (iv) Unfunded Collaborations

<Tip>
  Plain English: If a collaborator is helping but isn't getting paid out of this budget, describe their role in the **Facilities, Equipment and Other Resources** section and attach a **Letter of Collaboration** (not a letter of support) as supplementary documentation.
</Tip>

<Warning>
  Letters of collaboration must use the boilerplate format and stick to stating intent to collaborate. Letters of support (endorsements, enthusiasm letters) are not allowed unless explicitly required by the solicitation.
</Warning>

#### (v) Group Proposals

<Tip>
  Plain English: Multi-PI / interdisciplinary proposals still get only 15 pages. If you need more, you must get an approved deviation before submitting.
</Tip>

#### (vi) Proposals for Renewed Support

<Note>
  See Chapter V of the PAPPG for renewal-specific guidance.
</Note>
