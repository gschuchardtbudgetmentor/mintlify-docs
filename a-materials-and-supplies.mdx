## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **(a) Materials and Supplies (Line G.1)**
>
> When anticipated, the proposal budget justification must indicate the general types of expendable materials and supplies required.
>
> Supplies are defined as all tangible personal property other than those described in paragraph (d)(iii) above (Equipment).
>
> A **computing device is considered a supply** if the acquisition cost is less than the lesser of the capitalization level established by the proposer or **\$5,000**, regardless of the length of its useful life.
>
> In the specific case of computing devices, charging as a direct cost is allowable for devices that are **essential and allocable, but not solely dedicated**, to the performance of the NSF project.
>
> Details and justification must be included for items requested to support the project.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### (a) Materials and Supplies

<Tip>
  Plain English: Line G.1 is for **expendable stuff** — chemicals, glassware, small lab consumables, software licenses, small electronics, and any computer/device under \$5,000.
</Tip>

<Check>
  **Supplies = anything tangible that isn't Equipment (Line D)**:

  - Lab consumables (reagents, slides, gloves, pipette tips)
  - Field gear (sample containers, batteries)
  - Software licenses
  - Small electronics (sensors, Arduinos, cables)
  - Computers under \$5,000
</Check>

<Warning>
  The \$5,000 threshold for Equipment vs Supplies is fixed by NSF — but use your institution's capitalization threshold if it's lower than \$5,000.
</Warning>

#### Computing Devices Special Rule

<Tip>
  Plain English: A computer under \$5,000 is a supply (G.1), even if it's expected to last 5 years. To charge it directly as a supply, it must be:

  1. **Essential** to the project
  2. **Allocable** to the project
  3. **Not solely dedicated** to one project (it can be partially used elsewhere)
</Tip>

<Danger>
  Standard office laptops and general-purpose desktops are typically **unallowable** as direct charges even if under \$5,000 — they're considered general administrative cost recovered through indirect. Only project-essential computing belongs on G.1.
</Danger>

#### Justification Required

<Note>
  You don't need to list every pipette tip — "general lab consumables for cell culture work, estimated \$8,000/year" is acceptable. Group by category and explain WHY at the project level.
</Note>

<Check>
  Materials and supplies are included in the **MTDC base** — so they DO generate indirect costs (unlike Equipment).
</Check>
