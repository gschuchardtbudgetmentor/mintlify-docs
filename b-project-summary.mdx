## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

<Note>
  Skip to Part 2 for an annotated human readable version of this raw NSF Guidelines text.
</Note>

> ### **b. Project Summary**
>
> Each proposal must contain a summary of the proposed project not more than one page in length. The Project Summary consists of an overview, a statement on the intellectual merit of the proposed activity, and a statement on the broader impacts of the proposed activity.
>
> The overview includes a description of the activity that would result if the proposal were funded and a statement of objectives and methods to be employed. The statement on intellectual merit should describe the potential of the proposed activity to advance knowledge. The statement on broader impacts should describe the potential of the proposed activity to benefit society and contribute to the achievement of specific, desired societal outcomes.
>
> The Project Summary should be informative to other persons working in the same or related fields, and, insofar as possible, understandable to a broad audience within the scientific domain. It should not be an abstract of the proposal.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### b. Project Summary

Each proposal must contain a summary of the proposed project not more than one page in length.

<Tip>
  Plain English: One page. Hard limit. It's the first thing reviewers read and often the only thing some panelists read carefully before discussion.
</Tip>

The Project Summary consists of an overview, a statement on the intellectual merit of the proposed activity, and a statement on the broader impacts of the proposed activity.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Three required sections, each with its own heading:

  1. **Overview** — what you're going to do
  2. **Intellectual Merit** — why the science matters
  3. **Broader Impacts** — how it helps society
</Tip>

<Danger>
  All three headings must be present. Missing any one of them is grounds for NSF to return the proposal without review.
</Danger>

The overview includes a description of the activity that would result if the proposal were funded and a statement of objectives and methods to be employed.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Overview = what you'll do \+ why \+ how. Spell out objectives and methods, not just vague aspirations.
</Tip>

The statement on intellectual merit should describe the potential of the proposed activity to advance knowledge.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Intellectual Merit = will this push the field forward? Make the case that the science is original, important, and well-grounded.
</Tip>

The statement on broader impacts should describe the potential of the proposed activity to benefit society and contribute to the achievement of specific, desired societal outcomes.

<Tip>
  Plain English: Broader Impacts = beyond the science, what's the societal payoff? Education, workforce development, public engagement, outreach to underrepresented groups, partnerships, infrastructure, public policy, economic competitiveness, etc.
</Tip>

The Project Summary should be informative to other persons working in the same or related fields, and, insofar as possible, understandable to a broad audience within the scientific domain. It should not be an abstract of the proposal.

<Warning>
  Don't write a journal-style abstract here. Reviewers from outside your sub-field need to follow it. Cut jargon, define terms.
</Warning>

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### b. Project Summary

Each proposal must contain a summary of the proposed project not more than one page in length.

<Tip>
  Plain English: This is your one-page elevator pitch. Strict one-page max — no exceptions.
</Tip>

The Project Summary consists of an overview, a statement on the intellectual merit of the proposed activity, and a statement on the broader impacts of the proposed activity.

<Danger>
  The Project Summary MUST have three explicit, labeled sections: **Overview**, **Intellectual Merit**, and **Broader Impacts**. Proposals missing any of the three headings are returned without review.
</Danger>

The overview includes a description of the activity that would result if the proposal were funded and a statement of objectives and methods to be employed.

<Tip>
  Plain English: **Overview** = what you'll do, why, and how (objectives \+ methods).
</Tip>

The statement on intellectual merit should describe the potential of the proposed activity to advance knowledge.

<Tip>
  Plain English: **Intellectual Merit** = how this advances science/knowledge in your field.
</Tip>

The statement on broader impacts should describe the potential of the proposed activity to benefit society and contribute to the achievement of specific, desired societal outcomes.

<Tip>
  Plain English: **Broader Impacts** = how this benefits society — education, outreach, workforce development, inclusion, public engagement, economic benefits, etc.
</Tip>

The Project Summary should be informative to other persons working in the same or related fields, and, insofar as possible, understandable to a broad audience within the scientific domain. It should not be an abstract of the proposal.

<Warning>
  Don't just paste in a journal-style abstract. Reviewers and program officers from outside your subfield read this — write so a broader scientific audience can follow it.
</Warning>
