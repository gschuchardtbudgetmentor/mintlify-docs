## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### c. Proprietary or Privileged Information

<Tip>
  Plain English: If parts of your proposal contain trade secrets, sensitive commercial info, or other privileged material, you can flag it so NSF protects it from disclosure to outside reviewers and Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests — to the extent allowed by law.
</Tip>

#### How to mark proprietary content

<Check>
  1. **Check the "Proprietary or Privileged Information" box on the Cover Sheet**
  2. Clearly identify each portion that is proprietary (header/label or bracketed notation)
  3. Limit proprietary material to what is genuinely sensitive — not the whole proposal
  4. Follow specific submission instructions in Chapter II.E.1
</Check>

<Danger>
  If you don't check the Cover Sheet box, NSF has no obligation to treat the info as proprietary. Forgetting this step = the protection doesn't apply.
</Danger>

<Warning>
  Reviewers can decline to review a proposal containing extensive proprietary information — too much marked content makes review impossible. Mark sparingly.
</Warning>

<Note>
  Confidential budgetary information (senior personnel salaries) follows the same mechanism — see the Confidential Budgetary Information section of the budget instructions.
</Note>
