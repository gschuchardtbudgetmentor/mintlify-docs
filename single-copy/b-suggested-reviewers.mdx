## Information Key

## **<u>Part 1: Text Quote Directly from PAPPG (NSF 24-1)</u>**

<Check>
  NSF 24-1 applies to all proposals submitted or due on or after May 20, 2024.
</Check>

> ### **b. List of Suggested Reviewers or List of Reviewers Not to Include (optional)**
>
> Proposers may include a list of suggested reviewers (including email address and organizational affiliation) who they believe are especially well qualified to review the proposal. Proposers also may include a separate list of persons they would prefer not to review the proposal. These suggestions are optional. Exhibit II-2 contains information on conflicts of interest that may be useful in preparation of these lists.
>
> The cognizant Program Officer handling the proposal considers the suggestions and may contact the proposer for further information. The decision regarding whether to use these suggestions, however, remains with the Program Officer.

## **<u>Part 2: PAPPG Translated Into Plain English</u>**

### b. Suggested / Not-to-Include Reviewer Lists

<Tip>
  Plain English: You can optionally suggest reviewers who are well-qualified, and separately list people you'd prefer not to review your proposal. Both lists are optional, confidential, and Program Officer-discretionary.
</Tip>

#### What to include

<Check>
  For each suggested reviewer:

  - Name
  - Email address
  - Organizational affiliation
  - Brief rationale (optional but helpful)

  For the "not to include" list:

  - Name
  - Reason (e.g., scientific competitor, recent collaborator, personal conflict)
</Check>

<Warning>
  Don't suggest people from your COA Tables 3 or 4 (recent collaborators, advisors, advisees) — they'll be conflicted out anyway. Suggest external, qualified researchers who _don't_ have a conflict.
</Warning>

<Note>
  The Program Officer is **not obligated** to use any of your suggestions, but they often consider them — especially the "do not include" list.
</Note>

<Danger>
  Don't try to manipulate review. Stacking the suggested list with friends or padding the do-not-include list with anyone qualified to critique your work is obvious and counterproductive.
</Danger>
